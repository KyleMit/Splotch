# Provisional recommendation (2026-11-10, ~T+4.5 h; updated ~T+39 h)

**Verdict: keep shipping (SvelteKit + Capacitor) for now. Confidence: about 65%** under the owner's
best-bones lens (below), up from 60% after the real-iPad stall run (T+34.5 h). It had come down to
60% from 65–70% at T+19.5 h, because both of RN's suspected design floors turned out to be bugs with
validated fixes: the memory leak and slow Undo. No floor has been found on either side beyond the
accepted app size, and RN has not yet shown a user win, so the tie rule still decides it. Codex
round 3 (T+31 h) agrees at about 60%. The two overnight spikes (T+33 h) cancel out: each arm's stall
around Save comes from its own export code and is fixable in about 2–3 agent-days, so neither found
a floor. Picture durability (T+34.5 h) does not separate them either: both lose the picture in
progress on every kill and corrupt nothing; RN's slower export loses the gallery copy for longer,
weakly toward shipping, on an item already open. The real-iPad stall run (T+34.5 h) moved it: on the
device weighted most, shipping showed no detected loss or bend after Save or an auto-saving Clear (0
of 36 trials hit), while RN lost every stroke started 0.1–0.7 s after Save and inked 0.34–0.77 s
late after the Clear. Codex sized this outcome at 10–20 points toward shipping. It moves only 5,
because RN's Save loss has a gated fix; its late ink needs further unbuilt iOS work. Codex round 4
(T+35 h) agrees with the move and the number. Battery and heat on the Galaxy (T+39 h) leave it at
65%: drawing costs the same in both, shipping runs 1–3 °C warmer at the chip without throttling, and
stock React Native burns about a fifth of a core while RN idles in front. Codex round 4 sized "no
material difference" at 0–3 points. It was 60% when Claude and Codex first reached it independently,
65–70% after shipping's multi-finger result and the pricing of RN's known repairs, and 75% once the
Galaxy lane's four RN-only defects were counted. The owner then asked for the app with the best
bones: a fixable defect is repair cost, not a verdict. That counts RN's long bug list for less, so
the number comes back down until the floors and the unclear items are known. Round 3 took it from
60–65% to about 60%: several "fixable" labels had no passing fix, and the real-iPad drawing result
is inconclusive rather than a tie. The number is judgement, not a measured probability.

**Best-bones lens (owner, 2026-11-10 ~08:45).** Each finding is a floor, fixable (a fix passed its
gate) or unclear. The verdict now turns on:

* the iPad drawing floor. **Real iPad, frozen builds (T+25.5 h): no RN win under the pre-registered
  rule, but inconclusive rather than a tie.** The dense Crayon block had no scored pairs (both arms'
  controls failed), so it could only report presentation: RN presents at ~116 fps with a 108–117 ms
  stall in 2 of 3 runs, shipping at ~51 fps with none. RN's higher frame rate is a plausible benefit
  and its stalls a plausible cost; neither measures ink latency or feel (owner cases 2–3). Every RN
  defect found elsewhere repeats on the real iPad and none is new (`ipad-experiment.md`). The
  accumulated session (block 6) is clean in both arms;
* whether RN's memory growth is a leak or a plateau, and whether it can be bounded. This is the top
  priority, because a crash in front of a toddler is the worst outcome. **Answered for the leak
  (T+19 h): a bug, and fixed.** The frozen build leaks in a 32-minute session (peak 926 MB); the
  best fix plateaus (peak 580 MB, well under the smallest supported devices' estimated kill points).
  **Settled at T+20.5 h:** with the Undo fix and two memory purges added, memory reaches a ceiling
  and stays there (peak 527 MB, 443 MB after a 32-minute session, against shipping's 392 and 336).
  That remaining overhead is a bounded disadvantage whose irreducible part is unclear: the
  JavaScript heap sizes itself to drawing's garbage, and shrinking it costs collection time on the
  thread that inks, but trimming the drawing path's allocations and the probe's own share are
  untried (`rn-memory-bones.md`). **Real iPad, best versions (T+30 h): a ceiling too, and no
  warnings.** RN climbs while the paper fills, then sits flat at ~690–720 MB for 20 minutes through
  Clears and Undo; shipping peaks at 512 MB over four processes. No memory warning or kill in either
  arm, and RN's peak would use at most about half of a 2 GB iPad's limit (`ipad-memory.md`);
* whether RN's Undo can be made fast within the memory budget. **Answered (T+19.5 h): a bug.** Tile
  snapshots take Undo from 621 to 79 ms (p90) within 22.5 MB, byte-identical to a full replay
  (`rn-undo-bones.md`);
* whether RN's lost strokes come from small fixable slips or from the JS-thread design. **Galaxy
  stall lane, best versions (T+30.5 h): both.** Undo, resume and a dialog closing cost nothing new.
  Save loses every stroke started within ~1.6 s, through a fourth input lock of the kind already
  fixed for Clear (a slip). Both exports also delay ink 0.3–1.2 s because they run on the JavaScript
  thread: the design showing, but on work that could move off it, so unclear until a spike tries
  (`stall-ink.md`). **Spike (T+33 h): no floor found.** Saving from frozen ink keeps every stroke
  (27 of 27, was 0 of 27), and moving the export's pixel work to a worker thread cut the inking
  thread's longest busy stretch from 876 to 86 ms. Ink after Save is on time; after an auto-saving
  Clear 35 of 36 lines meet the 100 ms line (one at 108 ms), so it stays "unclear" by the
  pre-registered rule, with the residual named and movable. The worker's own memory is unmeasured
  (`spike-rn-export.md`). **Real iPad (T+34.5 h): both repeat.** Every stroke started 0.1–0.7 s
  after Save is lost (27 of 27 lines), and after an auto-saving Clear every line lands with its
  shape but 0.34–0.77 s late (27 of 27). iOS also composes the export's ink on the main thread
  (74–92 ms gaps), so the worker fix needs an iOS port too (`ipad-stall-ink.md`);
* shipping's WebView floors: extra fingers during a main-thread stall, and audio while muted.
  **Galaxy (T+30.5 h):** natural stalls bend strokes started within ~0.5 s of Save into straight
  lines (18 of 18), one finger as well as two, and Android killed shipping once for not responding
  in 69 auto-saving Clears, losing the drawing (0 in 48 repeats). Whether that hang is a floor
  depends on its rate. **Spike (T+33 h): the bending is shipping's own export code, not WebView.**
  On a 3× Android screen every export replays the whole drawing on WebView's single GPU thread (~0.4
  s), and every frame waits on it. A ~2-day fix that exports the live 2× tiles on the CPU
  straightens every probe line (36 of 36) and makes the saved picture arrive faster (331 against 767
  ms). The cost is small: saved stroke edges come from 2× pixels, visible only zoomed (3.1% of
  pixels off by more than 8 levels, against a pre-registered 1%). The iPad's 2× screen never takes
  that path (`spike-shipping-export.md`). **Real iPad (T+34.5 h): no loss or bend detected in the
  tested moments.** No bent, cut or lost stroke after Save or an auto-saving Clear (0 of 36 trials,
  54 probe lines on shape), and the app's touch log never paused more than 18 ms. The 60 ms chord
  control was not flagged, so drops shorter than ~120 ms could go unseen, and shipping's first-ink
  time is unmeasured (`ipad-stall-ink.md`)

The breadth pass found **no clear RN advantage** for users on any platform. That is not the same as
proving the arms equal: the most heavily weighted measurement, iPad drawing under real touch, shows
no RN win either, but its dense-picture block could only measure freezes, not how the ink feels
(owner cases 2–6 cover that). Under the campaign's rule RN has to show a user win to pay for its
extra upkeep, and it has not shown one yet. Day 1's strongest pro-RN signal, shipping losing
multi-finger ink on the iPad, turned out to be mostly an artefact of scripted touch on the
simulator. With real fingers, what is left is cosmetic, and a 10-line fix removes it.

The Galaxy brushes lane (T+8.5 h) tied on dense Crayon and Magic, and it showed the jank counter
does catch a known stall, so the Galaxy smoothness tie stands. Every new user-visible difference it
found is an RN defect, and three of the four lose ink or taps. The two lost-ink paths also show on
the iPad simulator, RN only (`ipad-input-loss.md`).

This is a judgement about the frozen candidates (RN `427756f14`, shipping `ef3d1eb20`). Repairs that
fix tests validate are reported separately and are never credited as measured quality. Lane notes in
this folder carry the methods, devices and raw evidence paths.

* **Lost contact** (ink that never appears): after Clear with Auto-Save on, after Clear right after
  an Eraser lift, and right after closing Settings.
* **Memory growth** at least twice shipping's: with Undo, and with plain drawing over a long
  session.
