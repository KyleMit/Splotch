# Provisional recommendation (2026-11-10, ~T+4.5 h; updated ~T+30.5 h)

**Verdict: keep shipping (SvelteKit + Capacitor) for now. Confidence: about 60–65%** under the
owner's best-bones lens (below). It came down from 65–70% at T+19.5 h, because both of RN's
suspected design floors turned out to be bugs with validated fixes: the memory leak and slow Undo.
No floor has been found on either side beyond the accepted app size, and RN has not yet shown a user
win, so the tie rule still decides it. Not yet through a Codex round. It was 60% when Claude and
Codex first reached it independently, 65–70% after shipping's multi-finger result and the pricing of
RN's known repairs, and 75% once the Galaxy lane's four RN-only defects were counted. The owner then
asked for the app with the best bones: a fixable defect is repair cost, not a verdict. That counts
RN's long bug list for less, so the number comes back down until the floors and the unclear items
are known. The number is judgement, not a measured probability.

**Best-bones lens (owner, 2026-11-10 ~08:45).** Each finding is a floor, fixable (a fix passed its
gate) or unclear. The verdict now turns on:

* the iPad drawing floor. **Real iPad, frozen builds (T+25.5 h): no RN win under the pre-registered
  rule.** On the dense Crayon picture RN presents at ~116 fps with a 108–117 ms stall in 2 of 3
  runs, shipping at ~51 fps with none. Every RN defect found elsewhere repeats on the real iPad and
  none is new (`ipad-experiment.md`). The accumulated session (block 6) is clean in both arms;
* whether RN's memory growth is a leak or a plateau, and whether it can be bounded. This is the top
  priority, because a crash in front of a toddler is the worst outcome. **Answered for the leak
  (T+19 h): a bug, and fixed.** The frozen build leaks in a 32-minute session (peak 926 MB); the
  best fix plateaus (peak 580 MB, well under the smallest supported devices' estimated kill points).
  **Settled at T+20.5 h:** with the Undo fix and two memory purges added, memory reaches a ceiling
  and stays there (peak 527 MB, 443 MB after a 32-minute session, against shipping's 392 and 336).
  That remaining overhead is a small floor: the JavaScript heap sizes itself to drawing's garbage,
  and shrinking it costs collection time on the thread that inks (`rn-memory-bones.md`). **Real
  iPad, best versions (T+30 h): a ceiling too, and no warnings.** RN climbs while the paper fills,
  then sits flat at ~690–720 MB for 20 minutes through Clears and Undo; shipping peaks at 512 MB
  over four processes. No memory warning or kill in either arm, and RN's peak would use at most
  about half of a 2 GB iPad's limit (`ipad-memory.md`);
* whether RN's Undo can be made fast within the memory budget. **Answered (T+19.5 h): a bug.** Tile
  snapshots take Undo from 621 to 79 ms (p90) within 22.5 MB, byte-identical to a full replay
  (`rn-undo-bones.md`);
* whether RN's lost strokes come from small fixable slips or from the JS-thread design. **Galaxy
  stall lane, best versions (T+30.5 h): both.** Undo, resume and a dialog closing cost nothing new.
  Save loses every stroke started within ~1.6 s, through a fourth input lock of the kind already
  fixed for Clear (a slip). Both exports also delay ink 0.3–1.2 s because they run on the JavaScript
  thread: the design showing, but on work that could move off it, so unclear until a spike tries
  (`stall-ink.md`);
* shipping's WebView floors: extra fingers during a main-thread stall, and audio while muted.
  **Galaxy (T+30.5 h):** natural stalls bend strokes started within ~0.5 s of Save into straight
  lines (18 of 18), one finger as well as two, and Android killed shipping once for not responding
  in 69 auto-saving Clears, losing the drawing (0 in 48 repeats). Whether that hang is a floor
  depends on its rate.

The breadth pass found **no clear RN advantage** for users on any platform. That is not the same as
proving the arms equal: the most heavily weighted measurement, iPad drawing under real touch, shows
no RN win either, but its dense-picture block could only measure freezes, not how the ink feels
(owner cases 2–6 cover that). Under the campaign's rule RN has to show a user win to pay for its
extra upkeep, and it has not shown one yet. Day 1's strongest pro-RN signal, shipping losing
multi-finger ink on the iPad, turned out to be mostly an artefact of scripted touch on the
simulator. With real fingers, what is left is cosmetic, and a 10-line fix removes it.

The Galaxy brushes lane (T+8.5 h) tied on dense Crayon and Magic, and it showed the jank counter
does catch a known stall, so the Galaxy smoothness tie stands. Every new user-visible difference it
found is an RN defect, and three of the four lose ink or taps. The two lost-ink paths also show on
the iPad simulator, RN only (`ipad-input-loss.md`).

This is a judgement about the frozen candidates (RN `427756f14`, shipping `ef3d1eb20`). Repairs that
fix tests validate are reported separately and are never credited as measured quality. Lane notes in
this folder carry the methods, devices and raw evidence paths.

## Flip risks

Repair cost is compared symmetrically: a shipping defect does not have to be unfixable to count for
RN, only more expensive to fix than RN's equivalent.

Resolved: *the simulator's 55% cuts showed a major real-finger defect.* Scripted touch on the
simulator arrives in one burst, and WebKit keeps only the last point of each extra finger; with
paced input the cuts disappear (0 of 50). Still open: *shipping keeps multi-finger fidelity in the
installed app*. The real-finger evidence is two Safari recordings, not the frozen app, and samples
for extra fingers during a main-thread stall stay unrecoverable even after the tail fix. Owner case
4 checks it.

**Toward RN**

1. **The fair iPad drawing pair under real touch.** One pre-registered route to an RN win: shipping
   freezes for 100 ms or more in at least 4 of 6 dense-Crayon runs, RN never does, and RN cuts lost
   in-contact time by at least half in every pair. Other measured user benefits count too. Even then
   RN carries the repair bill below before it can ship. *Check:* needs owner case 1.

   A second route opened at T+30.5 h: **shipping's export hang.** On the Galaxy Android killed
   shipping once in 69 auto-saving Clears, after WebView's GPU thread stuck in the graphics driver,
   and the drawing was lost. Above ~1% of exports it is a crash-class floor candidate (WebView plus
   the GPU driver), the first floor that would count toward RN. *Check:* a Galaxy export soak.

**Toward a firm no-go**

2. **RN's repair bill keeps growing.** RN's priced repairs now come to ~9–15.5 agent-days against
   shipping's ~1, and both guardrails break in more than one place. Neither design-level suspect is
   a floor any more: slow Undo is a bug (tile snapshots, validated), and the memory leak is a bug
   whose fix held for 32 minutes. What remains is a ceiling about 110 MB above shipping's on the
   Galaxy and about 210 MB at idle on the iPad, a small floor rather than growth. The three lost-ink
   paths share one trait: RN decides a contact's fate when it lands, so anything still holding the
   paper at that moment (a read-back under the input lock, a dialog window not yet taken down)
   silently eats the stroke. The iPad simulator shows the same two losses, RN only, so they are not
   Android quirks. Their fixes are small, though, so they add to the bill rather than settle the
   call. *Check:* the real-iPad run's block 7 repeats both (exploratory, not scored).

Still unknown and able to move either way: shipping's native AI success and saving (RN's works on
the Galaxy; shipping is untried because the test builds share one spent free grant), the rate of
shipping's export hang, a low-end device, and store distribution.

**Out of the decision (owner, 2026-11-10):** screen readers. A first TalkBack try found likely
shared defects, so a human pass on the winning app is issue 3226. Night Mode not recolouring
existing ink is by design (ADR-0052), not a shipping defect.
