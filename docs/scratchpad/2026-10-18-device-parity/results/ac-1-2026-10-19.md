## Rule applied

The oracle is the expected value a test compares the RN side against. A row qualifies when its named
tests take that value from shipping: an import from `web/src`, a read of shipping source or assets,
shipping code executed in the test, or a fixture derived from shipping whose derivation is itself
checked. A test that compares RN with itself, or with a hand-written constant, does not count. A row
whose module owns a rule (a branch, a count or a calculation) that the tests check only against
hand-written expectations does not qualify, even when its constants are read from shipping. A
one-way check, such as "every RN step appears in the shipping markup", does not qualify for content
whose order and completeness matter.

## What this record cannot establish

* **Behaviour on a device.** AC-1 confirms a logic module's contract against shipping source. A
  verified logic row says nothing about the behaviour rows that depend on it, which still need their
  protocols on simulators, emulators, browsers or physical devices.
* **Coverage beyond the named tests.** A verified row's tests hold the values and rules listed
  above. They are not a proof that every line of the module matches.
* **Staleness.** Each verified row is tied to the source above. `source-ownership.csv` marks it
  `stale` when a later head changes a path it depends on.
* **Container versus CI.** The per-test titles come from the container rerun. CI shows per-file
  results only. The two agree on every named file.
* **Group (c) reasons.** Apart from the 25 inspected rows, they quote the historical ledger and were
  not re-checked at this head.
* **L0161.** The defect is source-proven, not observed on RN web. Issue #3008 asks for the fix and a
  two-sided test.
