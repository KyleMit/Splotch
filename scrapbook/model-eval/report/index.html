Read this first. Eight variants, 19 inputs across 10 categories, 152 real calls, $8.73. Zero safety refusals anywhere — including the safety__toysword pretend-play probe — so nothing here suggests a false-positive problem on ordinary art. One transient upstream 500.

The latency finding decides the architecture. The line that matters is 24 s, not Netlify's 26 s ceiling: the handler aborts first on purpose so its own error wins the race (ADR-0063), so GENERATE_DEADLINE_MS is the wall a real request hits. Against it, all six OpenAI variants exceed the deadline at p90 and five of eight exceed it at the median, while both Gemini variants finish in about 7 s with room to spare. Even the fastest OpenAI tier — gpt-image-1-mini · low, 19.7 s median — is at 26.8 s by p90. There is no effort tier that keeps a buffered request/response handler viable, so moving generation to a start-then-poll flow is a requirement of the migration, not an optimization of it.

Cost goes down, not up, at the low end. gpt-image-1-mini · low is $0.0099/image — a quarter of what the current Gemini production model costs ($0.0389). Today's spend is not the constraint the tier choice should be made on.

Recommended: gpt-image-2 · medium ($0.058, 50.7 s median). It was the most consistent across the corpus — faithful to the child's own line colors, filling the frame, and composing a scene rather than reinterpreting the subject. It costs 1.5× the outgoing Gemini model, which is a real but small increase against a run rate this size. It is one named constant (IMAGE_QUALITY in web/src/lib/server/ai/openai.ts) if you disagree after looking at the gallery.

The two runners-up and why they lost: gpt-image-1.5 · medium is half the latency for the same money (25.1 s vs 50.7 s) and was excellent on line art, but washed out and badly composed on coloring-manual__cow. gpt-image-2 · high is not visibly better than medium on this corpus and costs 3.3× as much for 2× the wait.

Where to look in the gallery. coloring-manual__cow separates the field on fidelity: the child scribbled brown on the cow's back, and Gemini 2.5 rendered a black-and-white cow that ignored it, while gpt-image-2 · low went the other way and painted the scribble on literally as stripes. line__cat is the cleanest read on whether a model keeps the child's own stroke colors or repaints the subject — Gemini 3.1 and gpt-image-1-mini · medium both recolor the cat, the rest keep it. The OpenAI variants also fill the frame, where Gemini letterboxes a wide drawing into a bordered page.

Read the numbers with their limits. One sample per cell, and the run was made at concurrency 6, so these are throughput-under-load figures rather than an isolated single-call floor, and a single slow call moves a variant's median. That caveat matters for the cheapest tier, whose median sits just under the deadline; it does not rescue the recommended tier at 50.7 s.

Method

  • Inputs mirror what /api/generate-image receives — a flattened canvas of paper + coloring line art + the child's pen / magic-brush marks — built from the real web/static/coloring assets and the app's 15-color palette on the true paper colors. Regenerate with npm run model-eval:fixtures. Model-authored inputs carry a gen (filled) or line (stroke-only) prefix.
  • Every call sends the exact production request: DEFAULT_PROMPT and SAFETY_SYSTEM_INSTRUCTION, asserted byte-for-byte against the app source at runtime; default temperature.
  • The OpenAI variants run through the Responses API image-generation tool, not /v1/images/edits: only that shape accepts a real system instruction and lets the model decline in prose, which is what the app turns into its 422 safety refusal. tool_choice stays on auto so declining stays possible.
  • Cost = measured usage × published list rates. Image output is the dominant term (gemini-2.5-flash-image $30, gemini-3.1-flash-image $60, gpt-image-2 $30, gpt-image-1.5 $32, gpt-image-1-mini $8 per 1M image-output tokens); the OpenAI rows also carry the orchestrator's text tokens.
  • Latency was measured at concurrency 6 — calls overlapped, so treat these as throughput-under-load rather than an isolated single-call floor.
  • Full safety re-validation of the block-* corpus needs REDTEAM_FIXTURE_KEY and npm run redteam; this harness covers quality/cost/latency plus a pretend-play false-positive probe.
